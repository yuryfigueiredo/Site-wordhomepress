The quietest radio environment in the inner solar system isn't in a terrestrial desert—it is the permanently shadowed craters on the lunar far side. Free from Earth's ionosphere and human broadcast interference, long-wavelength radio telescopes deployed by robotic landers can detect 21-centimeter hydrogen signals from the era before the first stars ignited.

Listening Beyond the Ionosphere

On Earth, the ionosphere reflects radio waves below 10 megahertz back into space like a mirror. On the Moon’s far side, astronomers can finally map neutral hydrogen clouds from the first 100 million years after the Big Bang.
